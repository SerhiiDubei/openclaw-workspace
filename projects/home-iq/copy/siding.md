## MAIN ARTICLE: Siding Replacement Guide

### What You Need to Know About New Siding

Your home's siding is its first line of defense against weather, pests, and time. It also accounts for up to 40% of your home's curb appeal. Whether your current siding is damaged, outdated, or simply costing too much in maintenance, professional replacement offers immediate and long-term benefits.

**Why Siding Condition Matters**

Damaged or aging siding does more than look bad—it creates entry points for moisture that can destroy your home's structure from the inside out:
- **Water intrusion** leads to mold, rot, and compromised structural integrity
- **Gaps and cracks** increase heating and cooling costs by 10–20%
- **Pest entry** provides pathways for insects and rodents
- **Reduced resale value** from poor curb appeal and inspection findings

**Material Selection Guidance**

**Vinyl Siding (Most Popular)**
- Cost: $6–$12 per sq ft installed
- Pros: Affordable, low maintenance, 50+ color options
- Cons: Can crack in extreme cold, less impact-resistant than fiber cement
- Lifespan: 20–40 years

**Fiber Cement (Premium Durability)**
- Cost: $10–$18 per sq ft installed
- Pros: Fire-resistant, hail-resistant, looks like wood, 30–50 year lifespan
- Cons: Higher upfront cost, heavier (requires experienced installers)
- Best for: Long-term homeowners, severe weather areas

**Engineered Wood**
- Cost: $8–$14 per sq ft installed
- Pros: Authentic wood appearance, lighter than fiber cement, eco-friendly
- Cons: Requires some maintenance, not as widely available
- Best for: Historic homes, specific architectural styles

**Traditional Wood**
- Cost: $12–$22 per sq ft installed
- Pros: Authentic, customizable, repairable
- Cons: High maintenance (painting every 5–7 years), susceptible to rot/pests
- Best for: Historical accuracy, homeowners willing to maintain

### The Installation Process

**Phase 1: Inspection & Prep (1–2 days)**
- Remove old siding
- Inspect sheathing and framing for damage
- Replace damaged substrate
- Install house wrap and flashing

**Phase 2: Installation (3–10 days)**
- Install starter strips and corner posts
- Hang siding panels or boards
- Install trim around windows, doors, and corners
- Seal all penetrations and joints

**Phase 3: Finishing (1 day)**
- Final caulking and sealing
- Cleanup and magnet sweep for nails
- Final walkthrough with homeowner

### How much does siding replacement cost?

Professional siding replacement typically ranges from **$5,560 to $17,710** for an average home, with national averages around **$12,000–$14,000**.

**Cost breakdown by material (per sq ft installed):**
| Material | Price Range | Notes |
|----------|-------------|-------|
| Vinyl (basic) | $6–$9 | Most affordable option |
| Vinyl (insulated) | $8–$12 | Better energy efficiency |
| Engineered wood | $8–$14 | Wood look, lower maintenance |
| Fiber cement | $10–$18 | Premium durability |
| Traditional wood | $12–$22 | High maintenance but authentic |
| Stone/brick veneer | $18–$35 | Accent or full coverage |

**Factors affecting your total cost:**
- **Home size and stories** — Two-story homes cost more due to scaffolding/lift needs
- **Surface area** — Complex architecture with lots of corners increases material and labor
- **Removal complexity** — Multiple layers of old siding, asbestos, or lead paint add cost
- **Substrate condition** — Rotting sheathing or damaged framing requires repair
- **Trim and soffit work** — Often needed alongside siding replacement
- **Geographic location** — Labor costs vary significantly by region

**Example project costs:**
- 1,500 sq ft single-story (vinyl): $9,000–$13,500
- 2,500 sq ft two-story (fiber cement): $25,000–$45,000
- 3,000 sq ft with trim replacement (engineered wood): $24,000–$42,000

**Payment options:**
- Cash/check (often 3–5% discount)
- Financing plans (0% interest promotions common)
- Insurance claims (for storm damage)
- Home equity loans or HELOCs

Always get 3+ quotes to understand your specific project's price range.

### What's the difference between vinyl and fiber cement siding?

Vinyl and fiber cement are the two most popular siding materials, but they differ significantly in cost, performance, and maintenance.

**Vinyl Siding**

*Composition:* PVC plastic panels

*Pros:*
- Most affordable option ($6–$12/sq ft)
- Never needs painting (color is through the material)
- Lightweight and easy to install
- Wide variety of colors and styles
- Low maintenance (just wash occasionally)

*Cons:*
- Can crack or warp in extreme temperature swings
- Less impact-resistant than fiber cement
- Melts in house fires or from reflected heat
- Can fade over 15–20 years in intense sun
- Some homeowners feel it looks "plastic"

*Best for:* Budget-conscious homeowners, rental properties, mild climates

---

**Fiber Cement Siding**

*Composition:* Cement, sand, and cellulose fibers (James Hardie is the best-known brand)

*Pros:*
- Extremely durable (30–50 year lifespan)
- Fire-resistant (won't burn or melt)
- Hail and impact resistant
- Looks like wood but without the maintenance
- Rot and pest resistant
- Holds paint 2–3x longer than wood

*Cons:*
- Higher cost ($10–$18/sq ft)
- Heavy material requires experienced installers
- Requires painting every 15–20 years (unless pre-finished)
- Installation creates silica dust (requires safety precautions)
- More complex installation = longer timelines

*Best for:* Long-term homeowners, severe weather areas, premium aesthetics

---

| Factor | Vinyl | Fiber Cement |

**Bottom line:** If you plan to stay in your home 15+ years and want the best long-term value, fiber cement is worth the premium. If you're on a tighter budget or plan to move within 10 years, quality vinyl offers excellent value.

### Do I need to replace all my siding, or can I repair sections?

It depends on the damage extent, siding age, and material type.

**When repair works:**

*Storm damage to one side*
- If a storm damaged just the west-facing siding, that section can be replaced
- Match existing siding color and style
- Insurance often covers this scenario

*Isolated issues*
- One rotted corner or damaged panel
- Small area of pest damage
- Recent installation (under 5 years) in otherwise good condition

*Color matching considerations*
- Vinyl fades over time—new panels may not match old
- Some manufacturers offer "fade-resistant" lines with better color retention
- Consider replacing entire walls for consistency

**When full replacement makes sense:**

*Age over 20 years*
- Most siding reaches end of useful life around 20–30 years
- Repairs become "patchwork" that looks inconsistent
- New materials offer better efficiency and warranties

*Multiple problem areas*
- If you have damage in 3+ locations, repair costs approach replacement
- Underlying issues (rot, poor installation) likely widespread

*Upgrade opportunity*
- Moving from builder-grade to premium material
- Adding insulation or house wrap for efficiency
- Changing color or style (repair locks you into existing look)

**Cost comparison:**
- Section repair: $500–$2,000 per area
- Full replacement: $5,000–$45,000 depending on home size

**Professional recommendation:** If your siding is over 15 years old and you have multiple damage areas, get quotes for both repair and replacement. The long-term value often favors replacement.

### Will new siding improve my home's energy efficiency?

Yes, new siding can significantly improve energy efficiency—especially if you choose insulated siding or add house wrap during installation.

**How siding affects energy use:**

**Insulated siding**
- Adds R-value (thermal resistance) of 2.0–4.0
- Creates thermal break between exterior and interior
- Reduces thermal bridging through wall studs
- Can lower heating/cooling costs by 10–20%

**House wrap installation**
- Acts as air barrier, reducing drafts
- Allows moisture to escape while blocking wind
- Often required by modern building codes
- Should be installed under any new siding

**Sealing gaps and penetrations**
- Old siding often has gaps that leak air
- New installation includes proper sealing around windows, doors, utilities
- Reduces "stack effect" (warm air escaping through gaps)

**Real-world savings:**
| Home Size | Estimated Annual Savings |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| 1,500 sq ft | $200–$400 |
| 2,500 sq ft | $350–$700 |
| 3,500 sq ft | $500–$1,000 |

*Savings vary by climate, current insulation, and energy costs*

**Payback calculation:**
If insulated siding costs $3,000 more than standard and saves $400/year, payback is 7.5 years. After that, it's pure savings.

**Other efficiency considerations:**
- Siding replacement is a good time to add exterior wall insulation
- Window and door flashing should be updated
- Consider coordinating with attic insulation improvements
- Some utilities offer rebates for energy-efficient siding

**The bottom line:** While siding is primarily for protection and aesthetics, the efficiency gains are real and add up over time—especially in extreme climates.

### How do I choose the right siding contractor?

Siding is a major investment that should last 20–50 years. Choosing the right installer is as important as choosing the right material.

**Credentials to verify:**
- ✓ Licensed in your state (check online license databases)
- ✓ Liability insurance ($1M minimum)
- ✓ Workers' compensation insurance
- ✓ Manufacturer certifications (Hardie, CertainTeed, etc.)
- ✓ BBB accreditation with good rating

**Experience indicators:**
- 5+ years in business (10+ preferred)
- Specializes in siding (not general handyman)
- Experience with your chosen material
- Local references from the past 6 months

**Quote evaluation:**
- Detailed line-item breakdown
- Specific product names and grades
- Timeline with start and completion dates
- Warranty details (material and labor)
- Payment schedule (never 100% upfront)

**Red flags:**
- Door-to-door solicitation after storms
- Pressure to sign immediately
- Quotes 30%+ lower than others (cuts corners)
- No local address or verifiable business
- Requests cash payment or large upfront deposit
- Vague about materials or timeline

**Questions to ask:**
1. "What's your process for handling hidden damage?"
2. "Who will be on-site supervising?"
3. "How do you handle warranty claims?"
4. "What's your cleanup process?"
5. "Can you provide 3 recent local references?"

**The value of multiple quotes:**
Three quotes reveal the range of approaches and pricing. The lowest isn't always best—look for thoroughness, communication, and professionalism. The contractor who inspects your home most carefully often provides the most accurate quote.
