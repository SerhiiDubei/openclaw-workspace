## MAIN ARTICLE: Flooring Installation Guide

### What You Need to Know About New Flooring

New flooring can completely transform how your home looks and feels—but the results depend on three things: accurate measurements, quality materials, and proper installation. Whether you're replacing worn carpet, upgrading to hardwood, or choosing waterproof options for kitchens and baths, the right contractor makes all the difference.

**Why Measurement Matters**
Flooring isn't just about square footage. Professional installers measure for waste factors, seam placement, and transitions between rooms. DIY measurements often underestimate material needs by 10-15%, leading to costly reorder delays.

**Material Selection Guidance**
Different spaces need different floors:
- **Living areas:** Hardwood, engineered wood, or luxury vinyl plank
- **Kitchens/baths:** Waterproof LVP, tile, or sealed engineered hardwood
- **Basements:** Moisture-resistant options with proper subfloor prep
- **High-traffic zones:** Scratch-resistant surfaces with durable wear layers

**The Installation Process**
Most residential flooring projects follow this timeline:
1. **Pre-installation:** Furniture moved, old flooring removed, subfloor inspected
2. **Subfloor prep:** Leveling, patching, moisture barrier installation
3. **Installation:** Materials acclimated, laid according to manufacturer specs
4. **Finishing:** Trim installed, transitions placed, cleanup completed

### Benefits of Professional Flooring Installation

✓ **Refreshes any room** — Instant visual upgrade that increases home value  
✓ **Durable, easy-clean options** — Modern materials resist stains and scratches  
✓ **Waterproof choices available** — Perfect for kitchens, baths, and basements  
✓ **Safer walking surfaces** — Eliminate trip hazards from damaged or uneven flooring  
✓ **Proper subfloor prep** — Addresses underlying issues before they become expensive problems  
✓ **Warranty protection** — Professional installation maintains manufacturer warranties

## FAQ (Comprehensive)

### How much does flooring installation cost?

Professional flooring installation typically ranges from **$1,529 to $4,852** depending on several factors:

**Material costs (per sq ft installed):**
- Carpet: $3–$11
- Laminate: $6–$14
- Luxury vinyl plank (LVP): $7–$18
- Engineered hardwood: $8–$22
- Solid hardwood: $12–$28
- Tile: $13–$35

**Factors that affect your price:**
- **Room size and layout** — Complex shapes or multiple small rooms increase labor
- **Subfloor condition** — Repairs or leveling add $200–$800
- **Old flooring removal** — $1–$3 per sq ft, plus disposal
- **Furniture moving** — $50–$150 per room (or DIY to save)
- **Stairs, closets, transitions** — These details add time and materials

**Example project costs:**
- 500 sq ft living room (LVP): $3,500–$5,500
- 200 sq ft kitchen (tile): $2,600–$7,000
- Whole home (2,000 sq ft, engineered wood): $16,000–$28,000

The best way to get an accurate price? Get multiple quotes from local installers who can assess your specific space.

### How long does flooring installation take?

Most residential flooring projects are completed in **1–3 days**, but timelines vary:

**Typical installation times:**
| Project Type | Timeline | Notes |
|--------------|----------|-------|
| Single room (LVP/laminate) | 1 day | Fastest option |
| Single room (hardwood/tile) | 1–2 days | Includes acclimation time |
| Whole home (LVP) | 2–3 days | Sequential room installation |
| Whole home (hardwood) | 3–7 days | Depends on square footage |
| Stairs + multiple rooms | 2–4 days | More complex cuts and transitions |

**What affects the timeline:**
- **Material acclimation** — Hardwood needs 3–7 days to adjust to your home's humidity
- **Subfloor repairs** — Can add 1–2 days if leveling or patching is needed
- **Old flooring removal** — Carpet comes up fast; tile or glued flooring takes longer
- **Crew size** — Some companies send 2–3 installers, others send 4–6

**Pre-installation prep you can do:**
- Move small furniture and breakables
- Clear closets and under-bed storage
- Arrange pet/child care for installation days
- Ensure parking access for the work crew

Most contractors provide a detailed timeline during your quote so you know exactly what to expect.

### What's included in a flooring installation quote?

A comprehensive flooring quote should include these line items:

**Materials:**
- Flooring product (specific brand, style, color)
- Underlayment or padding
- Moisture barriers (if needed)
- Transitions, trim, and moldings
- Adhesives, fasteners, and supplies

**Labor:**
- Old flooring removal and disposal
- Subfloor preparation and repairs
- New flooring installation per manufacturer specs
- Trim and transition installation
- Final cleanup and walkthrough

**Additional services (may be extra):**
- Furniture moving and replacement
- Appliance disconnect/reconnect
- Baseboard removal and reinstallation
- Stain and finish matching (for hardwood)

**What to watch for in quotes:**
- **Vague line items** — "Miscellaneous materials" without specifics
- **Missing prep work** — Subfloor issues discovered during install can add hundreds
- **Warranty details** — Both material and labor warranties should be specified
- **Payment terms** — Never pay 100% upfront; 50/50 or thirds is standard

Reputable contractors itemize everything and explain each charge. If a quote seems significantly lower than others, ask what's excluded.

### Should I choose hardwood, laminate, or luxury vinyl plank?

The right flooring depends on your room, lifestyle, and budget. Here's how they compare:

**Solid Hardwood**
- ✓ Authentic, timeless appearance
- ✓ Can be refinished multiple times (20+ year lifespan)
- ✓ Increases home resale value
- ✗ Expensive ($12–$28/sq ft installed)
- ✗ Susceptible to moisture and scratches
- ✗ Requires professional refinishing every 7–10 years

**Best for:** Living rooms, dining rooms, bedrooms (above-grade only)

---

**Engineered Hardwood**
- ✓ Real wood veneer with plywood core
- ✓ More stable than solid wood (less expansion/contraction)
- ✓ Can be refinished 1–2 times
- ✓ Mid-range price ($8–$22/sq ft)
- ✗ Still vulnerable to deep scratches and moisture
- ✗ Limited refinishing compared to solid

**Best for:** Any room except baths; can work in basements with proper moisture barrier

---

**Luxury Vinyl Plank (LVP)**
- ✓ Waterproof and scratch-resistant
- ✓ Realistic wood/stone appearance
- ✓ Easy DIY installation option (click-lock)
- ✓ Affordable ($7–$18/sq ft installed)
- ✓ Soft underfoot, warmer than tile
- ✗ Can't be refinished (replace when worn)
- ✗ May fade in direct sunlight over time

**Best for:** Kitchens, baths, basements, rentals, high-traffic homes with kids/pets

---

**Laminate**
- ✓ Budget-friendly ($6–$14/sq ft)
- ✓ Scratch and fade resistant
- ✓ Easy click-lock installation
- ✗ Not waterproof (water-resistant options available)
- ✗ Can't be refinished
- ✗ Hollow sound/feel compared to real wood

**Best for:** Low-traffic areas, temporary solutions, rental properties

**Our recommendation:** For most homeowners, LVP offers the best balance of looks, durability, and price. If you're in your "forever home" and want maximum resale value, engineered hardwood is worth the investment.

### Do I need to move my furniture before installation?

**Furniture moving policies vary by contractor:**

**Option 1: Full-Service (usually $200–$500 extra)**
- Crew moves all furniture before installation
- Items placed in garage, other rooms, or covered on-site
- Furniture returned after installation complete
- Best for: Large homes, heavy items, homeowners with physical limitations

**Option 2: Partial Service**
- Crew moves large items (sofas, beds, appliances)
- Homeowner handles small items, decor, breakables
- Middle ground on cost ($100–$300)

**Option 3: DIY**
- Homeowner moves everything before crew arrives
- Free, but requires planning and labor
- Critical: Rooms must be completely empty including closet floors

**What to move yourself regardless:**
- Valuables, electronics, and fragile items
- Wall art and decor (vibration can cause falls)
- Items in closets and under beds
- Personal items from bathrooms

**Pro tip:** If doing DIY prep, move furniture 2–3 days early. This gives you time to clean baseboards, inspect the subfloor, and discover any issues (like that leak under the couch) before installation day.

### What is subfloor preparation and why does it matter?

The subfloor is the structural layer beneath your visible flooring. Its condition directly impacts how your new floors look, feel, and last.

**Common subfloor issues:**

**Unevenness**
- New flooring needs flat surface (typically within 3/16" over 10 feet)
- Uneven subfloors cause: bouncy floors, cracking in rigid materials, visible imperfections
- Solution: Self-leveling compound or plywood shims ($200–$800)

**Moisture problems**
- Excess moisture damages wood floors, promotes mold
- Causes: poor ventilation, plumbing leaks, ground moisture in basements
- Solution: Moisture barriers, dehumidifiers, addressing source leaks ($300–$1,500)

**Structural damage**
- Rot, termite damage, or sagging joists
- Often discovered when old flooring is removed
- Solution: Sistering joists, plywood replacement ($500–$2,000+)

**Why this matters for your quote:**
Subfloor issues can't always be detected during initial quote visits (especially with existing flooring in place). Reputable contractors:
- Include "allowances" for common prep work
- Explain potential additional costs upfront
- Document issues with photos when discovered
- Stop work if major problems are found (rather than covering them up)

**How to minimize surprises:**
- If possible, pull up a corner of old flooring before quotes
- Ask contractors specifically about subfloor assessment
- Get quotes from contractors with experience in homes your age
- Budget 10–15% contingency for subfloor work

Proper subfloor prep is invisible when done right—but very visible when skipped. It's worth the investment.

### How do I choose the right flooring contractor?

Beyond price, evaluate contractors on these factors:

**Credentials & Protection**
- ✓ Licensed in your state (verify online)
- ✓ Liability insurance ($1M+ coverage)
- ✓ Workers' compensation insurance
- ✓ Manufacturer certifications (for specific products)

**Experience & Specialization**
- How many years in business?
- Do they specialize in flooring or is it a side service?
- Experience with your specific material choice?
- Portfolio of completed projects similar to yours?

**Process & Communication**
- Detailed, written quote with line items?
- Clear timeline with start and end dates?
- Point of contact for questions during project?
- Cleanup and walkthrough process explained?

**Reviews & References**
- Recent reviews (last 6–12 months) on multiple platforms
- Response to negative reviews (professional or defensive?)
- Willing to provide 2–3 recent references?
- Photos of actual work (not just stock images)?

**Red flags to avoid:**
- Pressure to sign immediately ("price goes up tomorrow")
- Requests for large deposits (over 50%) or cash-only
- No physical address or verifiable business entity
- Quotes significantly lower than others (often excludes critical work)
- Vague answers about timeline or materials

**The value of multiple quotes:**
Getting 3 quotes isn't just about finding the lowest price—it's about understanding the range of approaches, materials, and expertise available. The contractor who asks the most questions, inspects most thoroughly, and explains their process clearly is often worth a modest premium.
